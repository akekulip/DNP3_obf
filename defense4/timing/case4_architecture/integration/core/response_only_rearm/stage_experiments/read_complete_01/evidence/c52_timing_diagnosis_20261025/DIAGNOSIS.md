## Undecidable from master-only data

The master NIC sees one end of a path that runs master -> switch (T) -> relay -> switch -> master. The following hypotheses each fit the tables above and cannot be separated without a second observation point:

1. **Relay first-response turnaround versus switch hold.** A slow first transaction on a connection can be the relay answering its first request late (cold application state), or T holding the ACK until readiness (30.000 ms from T's t0) and the response until its fallback. Decided by a passive tap on the relay leg (relay-side RX/TX timestamps of the same request, ACK and response), which shows when the relay itself produced the ACK and response.
2. **Where the CLRT error is added.** A CLRT that differs from the gap (950 us) can come from the relay releasing the ACK and response at its own spacing that T then does not reshape, from T's release scheduling, or from queueing on the return path. Decided by switch-side ingress/egress timestamps for the ACK and response (and the relay-leg tap for their arrival at the switch).
3. **Forward delay to T's t0.** The request->ACK interval includes the master->switch forward delay and any switch ingress delay before t0; neither readiness nor da can be checked against it. Decided by switch timestamps of request ingress (t0) or a tap on the master leg at the switch.
4. **Which transaction a fallback counter recorded.** The counters are per trial; the signature here is a master-side match only. Decided by per-transaction switch records (fallback events keyed by connection and generation).
5. **Cause of a request retransmission.** A missing ACK at the master before its RTO can be a request lost before the relay, a request the relay received but answered late, or an ACK held or dropped in the switch. Decided by the relay-leg tap (did the original request reach the relay, and when did the relay ACK it) together with switch drop/hold counters per packet.
