## Primary result

The fixed Random-Forest attacker uses joint features (request→ACK, CLRT, response length). It was trained on native
(Timing OFF, no padding) traffic of rounds 1–8 and applied unchanged. At D = 10 ms, J = 2.4 ms, over the 8 held-out
test blocks:

| | balanced accuracy |
|---|---|
| native (off) | 0.950 |
| combined (timing and size defence) | 0.333 (chance for three classes) |
| **reduction, native minus combined** | **0.617, conditional 95% interval [0.593, 0.635]** |

The interval comes from a paired block bootstrap: 10,000 resamples of the 8 matched block pairs, seed 20260828. The
resamples add numerical stability; they do not make the 8 test blocks into more experiments
(`results/paired_reductions.csv`, the `primary` row).

## What the numbers do and do not show

1. **Against the fixed attacker, the combined defence brings every condition to chance.** For READ/SELECT/OPERATE
   timing and response size, the attacker trained before deployment learns nothing from defended traffic.
2. **Part of that drop is distribution shift, not removed information.** The fixed attacker also falls to 0.333 on
   size_only traffic, yet an attacker retrained on size_only recovers 0.58–0.65. A fixed attacker meeting unfamiliar
   lengths predicts poorly whatever information remains. The adaptive figures are therefore the conservative
   measure of what is left.
3. **Residual leakage against a retrained attacker.** On combined traffic, the adaptive joint attacker reaches
   0.36–0.46 across the eight conditions, above chance in every one. The defence reduces leakage strongly but does
   not remove it for an attacker who retrains on defended traffic.
4. **The metadata diagnostic still identifies operations, at 0.69–0.80 on combined traffic.** It uses observed
   request and response lengths, packet counts and direction. Requests are native by design (no request padding),
   so the request side very likely carries the operation (an inference, not separately measured). Claims are scoped
   to response timing and size, as the protocol states.
5. **Windowed Naive Bayes (K = 64) rests on few windows** and uses operation-grouped (oracle-assisted) windows. Its
   values vary widely by condition and are not a strong attack estimate.
6. **These are transaction-class results for the evaluated attackers.** They are not device identification and do
   not generalise to all fingerprinting classifiers.

## Host-observed precision (reported, not gated)

Across the timed arms (timing_only, combined) of the test blocks:
- 24,704 exchanges, of which **99.705% are within 100 µs of D**;
- 73 exchanges fall outside;
- per arm, condition and operation: p50 error 4–30 µs, p99 0.045–1.027 ms, maximum 0.079–1.926 ms
  (`results/precision_summary.csv`, `results/precision_residuals.csv`, `results/figures/precision_full_tails.pdf`).

The tail is the known single-exchange host-observed delay, localized on m9d downstream of the switch's egress
parser. It is inherited, not new.

## Limitations carried forward

- **Restart path:** model-proven; not exercised on silicon.
- **m9 timing measurements:** inherited, not re-measured on m11_09.
- **Training lock:** replay checks the lock's hashes but does not refit the models to confirm them.
- **Runner exit code:** the rule that a hard failure or failed restore still exits 1 is covered by inspection, not
  by a test.
- **Raw evidence:** about 11 GB, kept on Gambit's disk and not committed (Philip, 2026-10-19). See
  `eval_m11_09_20261018_02_EVIDENCE_LOCATION.md`.
