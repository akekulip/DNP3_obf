\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we presented NOMAD, an in-network traffic obfuscation mechanism against ICS device fingerprinting over plain-text communication. NOMAD uses the syntax of control units to apply a different strategy to each type of operation on one programmable switch: it replaces the CLRT of READ and SELECT with a configured value, holds OPERATE commands inside the switch, and splits the control units of every response into one fixed layout. On a Tofino-1 testbed with 98,816 DNP3 transactions, the CLRT at the master stayed within 10--16~$\mu$s of the configured value at the median, and all responses carried 61~bytes and remained conformant to IEEE~1815. A classifier trained before deployment fell from 0.950 to 0.333 balanced accuracy, a reduction of 0.617 [0.593, 0.635]. A retrained classifier still reached 0.36--0.46, highest at $D$ = 10~ms, where READ and SELECT arrive 20--29~$\mu$s earlier than OPERATE, and 0.69--0.80 when it also uses the native requests. All transactions completed within 53~ms without retransmission. In future work, we will extend the strategies to requests and evaluate NOMAD with physical relays of several vendors.
