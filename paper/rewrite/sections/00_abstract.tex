\begin{abstract}
Adversaries preparing attacks on industrial control systems (ICSs) such as power grids first
perform reconnaissance, and device fingerprinting supplies much of the knowledge they need. In ICS
networks, the fingerprint does not have to come from the payload. The interval between the transport
acknowledgment of an outstation and its application response reflects the execution time of the
device, and it identifies both the device type and the operation the device performs. Existing
traffic obfuscation does not remove this interval, because it reshapes packet sizes and inter-packet
gaps of encrypted flows, whereas ICS traffic is unencrypted and any padding or dummy packet reveals
itself.

To overcome these challenges, we propose an in-network timing-obfuscation framework that replaces
the interval with a value the operator configures. A programmable switch placed in front of the
outstation holds the acknowledgment and the response and releases each of them at a deadline
computed from the same instant, such that the execution time of the device cancels from the interval
an adversary measures. The switch only delays the packets it receives, so both endpoints run
unmodified and no DNP3 byte changes. We implement the framework on an Intel Tofino-1 switch and
evaluate it against a physical SEL-751A protective relay over 63{,}360 DNP3 exchanges. The
experimental results show that, with negligible overhead, the framework can disrupt timing-based
fingerprinting. The released interval settles on the configured value, and the mutual information it
carries about the operation falls into its permutation null. A classifier trained on Timing OFF
traffic falls from 0.745 balanced accuracy to three-class chance, and an adaptive adversary that
pools twenty exchanges stays at chance, where Timing OFF traffic lets it reach 0.988. The framework
adds no frame and no byte, and its 22.7~ms of added latency stays well within the 201~ms after which
the master repeats an unacknowledged request.
\end{abstract}
