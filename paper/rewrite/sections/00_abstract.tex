\begin{abstract}
Adversaries preparing attacks on industrial control systems (ICSs) such as power grids first
perform reconnaissance, and device fingerprinting supplies much of the knowledge they need. In ICS
networks, the fingerprint does not have to come from the payload. The interval between the transport
acknowledgment of an outstation and its application response carries the execution time of the
device, and it identifies both the device and the operation it performs. Suppressing that timing variation is challenging when legacy endpoints must continue exchanging
readable protocol messages. We focus on changing the timing of those existing exchanges.

To address this problem, we propose an in-network timing-obfuscation framework that schedules
acknowledgments and responses around an interval the operator configures. A programmable switch in
front of the outstation holds the acknowledgment and the response and releases each of them at a
deadline computed from the arrival of the request. Packets arriving before their deadlines can
follow this schedule, subject to queue service and the supported holding range. The switch only delays the packets it receives, so both endpoints run unmodified and
no DNP3 byte changes. We implement the framework on an Intel Tofino-1 switch and evaluate it with a
physical SEL-751A protective relay over 63{,}360 DNP3 exchanges. The experimental results show that
the measured CLRT medians approach the configured 8~ms for READ, SELECT and OPERATE, while the
READ interquartile range falls from 2.772 to 0.022~ms. A classifier trained before deployment
falls from 0.803 balanced accuracy to chance, although an adaptive classifier retains accuracy
of 0.445. The median READ request-to-response time increases to 28.107~ms, with no retransmission
observed in the campaign.
\end{abstract}
