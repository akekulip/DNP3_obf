\begin{abstract}
Adversaries preparing attacks on industrial control systems (ICSs) such as power grids first
perform reconnaissance, and device fingerprinting supplies much of the knowledge they need. In ICS
networks, the fingerprint does not have to come from the payload. The interval between the transport
acknowledgment of an outstation and its application response reflects the execution time of the
device, and it identifies both the device and the operation it performs. Removing that interval is
challenging. Existing traffic obfuscation reshapes packet sizes and inter-packet gaps of encrypted
flows, whereas ICS traffic is unencrypted, and any padding or dummy packet reveals itself.

To overcome these challenges, we propose an in-network timing-obfuscation framework that replaces
the interval with a value the operator configures. A programmable switch in front of the outstation
holds the acknowledgment and the response and releases each of them at a deadline computed from the
same instant, such that the execution time of the device cancels from the interval an adversary
measures. The switch only delays the packets it receives, so both endpoints run unmodified and no
DNP3 byte changes. We implement the framework on an Intel Tofino-1 switch and evaluate it against a
physical SEL-751A protective relay over 63{,}360 DNP3 exchanges. The experimental results show that,
with negligible overhead, the framework can disrupt timing-based fingerprinting. A classifier
trained before deployment falls from 0.745 balanced accuracy to three-class chance, an adaptive
adversary that pools many exchanges stays at chance, and the framework adds no frame and no byte.
\end{abstract}
