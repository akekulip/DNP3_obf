\begin{abstract}
Device fingerprinting lets adversaries in industrial control systems (ICSs) learn which devices and operations they face before an attack. Existing traffic obfuscation pads, splits and delays packets over encrypted channels. However, ICS networks still carry plain-text protocols such as DNP3, where filler and dummy packets are visible, and ICS fingerprinting relies on the timing between different types of packets, e.g., the cross-layer response time (CLRT) between a TCP acknowledgment and the response. In this paper, we present NOMAD, an in-network traffic obfuscation mechanism that uses the syntax of control units to apply a different strategy to each type of operation on a programmable switch. NOMAD replaces the CLRT of reads with a configured value, holds control commands inside the switch, and splits the control units of every response into one fixed layout, without changing the endpoints or the meaning of any control unit. We implement NOMAD on an Intel Tofino-1 switch and evaluate it with 98,816 DNP3 transactions. The CLRT at the master stays within 10--16~$\mu$s of the configured value at the median, and all responses carry 61~bytes and remain conformant to IEEE~1815. A classifier trained before deployment falls from 0.950 to 0.333 balanced accuracy, i.e., chance, and all transactions complete within 53~ms without retransmission.
\end{abstract}
