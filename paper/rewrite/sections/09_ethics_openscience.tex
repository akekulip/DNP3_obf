\section*{Open Science}

We reproduce every number and every campaign figure in this paper from the raw captures with one
script, in a pinned environment archived with the extraction and analysis code. A hash manifest
covers each published artefact, and a publication gate compares a fresh rebuild against what the
paper reports, so a discrepancy fails rather than passing silently. We also publish the switch
program that ran, with its compilation record. The firmware of the relay and its vendor
documentation are not ours to distribute. On acceptance, we release the captures, the analysis
pipeline and the switch program under an open license.

\section*{Ethics Considerations}

This work required no human subjects and no personal data. We ran every experiment on a laboratory
testbed we own, namely one SEL-751A protective relay, one Intel Tofino-1 switch and one master
host, on an isolated network with no connection to any operational system. We contacted no
production grid, no utility network and no third-party device.

The relay is physical equipment whose outputs can operate a breaker. Every select-before-operate
exchange in the evaluation addresses isolated control points through a guarded driver. No
control operation in this paper moved a breaker or energized a circuit, and each OPERATE is a
protocol exchange the relay accepted and acknowledged.

The framework is defensive. It removes a timing feature that an adversary uses for reconnaissance,
and it neither creates a new attack nor discloses a vulnerability in the relay, the switch or the
DNP3 protocol.
