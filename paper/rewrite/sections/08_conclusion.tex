\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We propose an in-network framework that holds the
packets carrying the timing of an ICS device and releases them at deadlines computed from the
arrival of the request. For packets available before their deadlines, the schedule can absorb arrival-time variation.
Queue service, late arrivals and the supported holding range determine how closely the observed
intervals follow the configured values. The framework runs without encryption and leaves the master,
the outstation and DNP3 unchanged.

On a physical SEL-751A relay, CLRT medians for READ, SELECT and OPERATE approach the configured
8~ms, and the READ interquartile range falls from 2.772 to 0.022~ms. A classifier trained before
deployment falls from 0.803 balanced accuracy to chance, while an adaptive classifier retains
0.445 accuracy and reaches 0.650 when pooling twenty exchanges. Median READ request-to-response
time rises to 28.107~ms, with no retransmission observed in the campaign. The residual tests
associate timing information with request spacing but do not isolate its cause.

In future work, we will evaluate the framework on outstations from several vendors, and we will
investigate how blocker-queue state contributes to release timing.
