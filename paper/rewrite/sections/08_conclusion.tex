\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A passive observer of legacy DNP3 traffic can fingerprint an outstation from the timing of its
responses, and the size- and delay-based obfuscation built for encrypted Internet traffic does not
transfer to a plaintext control protocol on devices that cannot be changed. We presented a
timing-obfuscation framework that runs in the data plane of one programmable switch. It holds the
packets that carry an outstation's timing and releases them at configured offsets. One rule, for
polls and the SELECT phase of control, is anchored to the outstation's acknowledgment; the other,
for OPERATE, is anchored to the request. We implemented it as one P4 program on a Tofino-1 and evaluated it
against a physical SEL-751A relay. It pinned the CLRT of READ and SELECT to 4.001~ms with a
0.02~ms standard deviation, where the relay's own CLRT spread over 1 to 18~ms. It kept the
master-visible OPERATE timing at 4.00~ms across configured holds of 2, 6 and 12~ms. It reduced the
mutual information between transaction class and CLRT from 0.42~bits to inside its permutation
null, and a READ-versus-SELECT classifier fell from 0.59 balanced accuracy to chance. The
results show the suppression of a timing channel on one relay in one session. The function codes
stay visible, the relay-facing side was not observed, and size shaping was active in both arms of
the frozen evidence. The next validation is a rerun of the same protocol with the size datapath
disabled and a relay-facing tap, so that the timing mechanism is isolated outright and the
release at $T_0 + J$ is measured rather than configured.
