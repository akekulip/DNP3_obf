\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Instead of padding packets or adding dummy ones, we propose an in-network framework that holds the
packets carrying the timing of an ICS device and releases them on a schedule the operator sets.
Because both release deadlines are computed from one instant, the execution time of the device
cancels from the interval an adversary measures, which is the interval that cross-layer
response-time fingerprinting relies on. The framework runs without encryption and leaves the master, the outstation
and DNP3 unchanged.

On a physical SEL-751A relay, the released interval settles on the configured value, its
interquartile range falls from 2.778 to 0.006~ms, and the operator can place it anywhere from 1 to
22~ms. A classifier trained on Timing OFF traffic falls from 0.745 balanced accuracy to chance, and
an adaptive adversary that pools twenty exchanges, as fingerprinting methods do, stays at chance,
where Timing OFF traffic lets it reach 0.988. The framework adds no frame and no byte, and it
raises the median response time by 22.7~ms, well within every timer of TCP and DNP3. In future work, we will evaluate the framework on outstations from
several vendors and on intervals beyond the two we replace.
