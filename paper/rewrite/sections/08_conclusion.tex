\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Instead of padding packets or adding dummy ones, we propose an in-network framework that holds the
packets carrying the timing of an ICS device and releases them at deadlines computed from the
arrival of the request. Because neither release depends on anything the outstation does, the
execution time of the device reaches neither the request-to-acknowledgment interval nor the
cross-layer response time an adversary measures, which is the interval that cross-layer
response-time fingerprinting relies on. The framework runs without encryption and leaves the master,
the outstation and DNP3 unchanged.

On a physical SEL-751A relay, the released interval settles on the configured 8~ms for every
operation, and the interquartile range of READ falls from 2.772 to 0.022~ms. READ and SELECT, which
differ only in execution time, become indistinguishable, and a classifier trained before deployment
falls from 0.803 balanced accuracy to chance. An adaptive adversary reaches 0.445 by learning when
requests reach the switch, not how the relay executes them. The framework adds no frame and no byte,
and the median request-to-response time of a poll stays at 28.107~ms, well within every timer of TCP
and DNP3.

In future work, we will evaluate the framework on outstations from several vendors, and we will
make the release independent of the state of the blocker queue.
