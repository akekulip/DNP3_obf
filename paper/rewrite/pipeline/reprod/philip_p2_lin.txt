Industrial control systems run on old protocols. A device, its type, and its model can be read from the traffic it leaks. Because a control attack only works when the attacker knows the target, the attacker first learns which devices are on the wire and how they behave. Consequently, device fingerprinting is the first step of the intrusion. For example, in December 2015 intruders reached the Ukrainian power grid and left 225,000 users in the dark. Since prior systems such as Ditto pad every packet and add dummy packets, they hide size and timing at line rate. Unfortunately, these methods do not fit ICS traffic, because padding and chaff break the exchange the master checks.
