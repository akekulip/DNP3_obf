Release timeline of the read lane, drawn from the verified program and not from a distribution. Five instants appear, all at the switch: $t_0$ when the request arrives, $t_A$ and $t_R$ when the outstation's acknowledgment and response arrive, and $e_A$ and $e_R$ when each of them is released. The switch arms both deadlines from $t_0$, so the acknowledgment is due at $t_0+D_A$ and the response at $t_0+D_A+\mathrm{CLRT}_{\mathrm{new}}$. Neither instant depends on $t_A$ or $t_R$, so the relay's own cross-layer time $t_R-t_A$ does not reach the interval the adversary measures. That interval is $e_R-e_A$: the master-facing propagation is the same for both packets and cancels. The response hold $e_R-t_R$ is drawn in (a) to show that it is not configured but is whatever the schedule requires. In (b) the response arrives after its deadline, the program forwards it on arrival, no hold applies and none is drawn, and the measured interval exceeds the configured value. $D_A=20$ ms and a configured $\mathrm{CLRT}_{\mathrm{new}}=4$ ms are the campaign settings.
