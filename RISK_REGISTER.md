# Risk register

Risks to the fixed-transcript objective, ranked by how likely they are to force a bounded claim or a
no-go. Likelihood and impact are High/Medium/Low. Each risk names the mitigation and the experiment
or document that would retire it. This register is seeded by the lead and updated from the specialist
findings and the skeptical review before the decision memo is finalized.

|---|------|-----------|--------|-------------------------------|

| R2 | **Empty scheduled slot stalls the pattern.** If the TM is asked to emit a cell from an empty queue, the slot is skipped and the skip is an observable deviation. | Medium | High | The token-reservoir construction (Candidate E) keeps a chaff cell always available; `TOFINO_TM_FEASIBILITY.md` must confirm strict-priority-over-always-nonempty-chaff on silicon terms. | R3 | **Ingress resource saturation.** The live timing core already fills ingress stages 8-11 at 16/16 logical tables and the 32-bit PHV group at 100%. Adding transcript logic that lands on the tail may not fit. | Medium | High | `RESOURCE_BUDGET.md` budgets in logical-table IDs, not stages. Keep new work in egress / early stages where possible. | R10 | **Temptation to reuse falsified mechanisms.** Trailer padding below IP and adaptive CRC splitting are falsified/barred but convenient. | Low | High | Scope discipline in the charter; these are barred by rule. | R11 | **Request-direction channel left open.** Shaping only responses leaves request size leaking READ-vs-DIRECT_OPERATE. | High | Medium | A single outstation-edge switch cannot shape the request side alone; the paired-gateway design (Architecture 4) covers both directions. | R12 | **Scope creep into implementation.** A compiling probe is not a result; the risk is drifting from research into a build before the decision is made. | Medium | Medium | Charter phase discipline: no production pipeline this phase; compile probes are resource measurements only. |
| R13 | **TM cadence jitter makes the release grid unproven, and the proven Defense 4 primitive is the wrong mechanism for it.** Tofino-1's max-rate shaper clumps below ~600 pps (bursts after silence) while hitting the correct average; a fixed transcript is a cadence property, not an average-rate property. The alternative pktgen periodic timer (~100 pps) has an inter-packet-gap floor and jitter that are undocumented in the SDE. Critically, the silicon-proven Defense 4 result is **event-anchored deadline release** (`t_wire = min{τ_i ≥ t_eligible}`), a reactive per-transaction mechanism, **not** a free-running metronome that emits cells whether or not real data exists; the metronome is exactly what is unmeasured, and the D4 deadline result must not be cited as evidence the grid works. Until the grid is measured on the actual switch, `release_time` regularity, the whole point, is unproven. | High | High | `TOFINO_TM_FEASIBILITY.md`; `DECISION_MEMO.md` §8-10. Retire with a silicon cadence characterization of the pktgen periodic timer (jitter, floor, max clean rate) and a head-to-head against a software IP-TFS pacer, tied to the DNP3 latency budget. Decision-critical alongside R1: a negative result collapses the switch's unique role and forces the impossibility-plus-floor paper. |
| R14 | **Unsound novelty framing: "plaintext CRC-valid chaff, single-edge, endpoint-preserving."** The literature analysis proposes this as a novelty direction, but the transport safety analysis shows plaintext CRC-valid chaff is either detectable (bad CRC / illegal frame) or dangerous (the master's DNP3 stack runs `ProcessIIN` even on rejected frames; a fabricated CONFIRM can delete relay SOE records). A single-edge, no-encryption design provably cannot reach a fixed transcript. | Medium | High | Reconcile in the decision memo: the sound novelty is the paired-gateway encrypted design (Ditto-with-a-software-peer + IP-TFS-style fragmentation + DNP3-aware eligibility + CLRT normalization + edge header-scrub), not plaintext chaff. The single-edge no-crypto version is the Architecture-5 floor and is reported as an impossibility result, not a defense. |

## The three that decide the project

R1 (local box unavoidable), R13 (cadence jitter unproven), and R4 (header fingerprint) are the risks
that determine whether the objective is reachable at all.

- R1 is confirmed as a constraint, not a maybe: it converts Open Question 4 into the pivotal decision.
  If Philip can deploy a local encapsulation function, the path is the paired-gateway design with the
  switch as metronome and header-scrubber. If not, the honest outcome is a bounded claim covering
  timing and header shaping without full size/count/occurrence hiding (the Architecture-5 floor).
- R13 is the sharpest purely technical unknown: the defense's value is the regularity of the release
  grid, and that regularity is not yet measured on silicon. It is the first experiment to run if the
  decision is GO, and a poor cadence result alone can force a bounded claim or a no-go.
- R4 is closed only by the encrypted outer layer (R1) plus edge header rewrite; queue timing alone
  never touches it. The measurement specialist's first falsification experiment targets exactly the
  endpoint-stamped header fields that no single-edge design can rewrite.
